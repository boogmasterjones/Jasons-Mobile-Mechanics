        <h2>Pre-Purchase Car Inspection in Port Charlotte, FL</h2>
        <p>Buying a used car is one of the largest financial decisions most people make, and in Southwest Florida's active private-sale market — where snowbirds sell before heading north and local classifieds move vehicles quickly — there's rarely time for a leisurely shop evaluation. A pre-purchase inspection from Jason's Mobile Mechanics puts an independent mechanic at the seller's location to go over the vehicle before money changes hands.</p>
        <p>We check the things that aren't visible in photos or apparent in a short test drive: stored fault codes the seller may have cleared, brake pad thickness, transmission and differential fluid condition, suspension wear, fluid leaks, battery and alternator health, and structural indicators of accident history. You'll leave the inspection with a clear picture of what the vehicle is actually worth versus what the seller is asking — and what repairs, if any, you'd be inheriting.</p>
        <h3>Why Get an Independent Inspection?</h3>
        <ul>
          <li>A Carfax report shows accident history but doesn't tell you if repairs were done correctly</li>
          <li>Dealer inspections are done by the same party who benefits from selling you the car</li>
          <li>Cosmetically clean vehicles routinely have hidden mechanical problems in Florida's humidity and heat</li>
          <li>You get an upfront quote before the inspection begins</li>
        </ul>
        <h3>Florida-Specific Issues We Look For</h3>
        <p>Buying a used car in Southwest Florida carries risks that buyers from cooler climates don't always anticipate. Vehicles that have spent years in the region often show evidence of salt air exposure in the undercarriage — rust on brake lines, fuel lines, and suspension components that can be difficult to spot from above but clearly visible during a ground-level inspection. Vehicles that have lived near Charlotte Harbor, Lemon Bay, or the Gulf waterfront are especially prone to accelerated corrosion, even on models that look cosmetically clean. We inspect the undercarriage specifically for this kind of damage on every pre-purchase inspection.</p>
        <p>Florida also has a large population of seasonal residents who put very low annual mileage on their vehicles — which sounds like a positive but can mean aged rubber that has cracked from sitting in heat, brake rotors that have developed surface rust from non-use, fuel systems that have seen multiple seasons of ethanol-blended fuel sitting in the tank, and batteries that have been repeatedly discharged and surface-charged without ever being properly conditioned. We treat low-mileage Florida vehicles with the same scrutiny as high-mileage ones, because the failure modes are different but equally real and expensive after purchase.</p>
